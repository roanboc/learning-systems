How this world works

Not real chemistry. Six made-up kinds of molecule with simple local rules. Real atoms would be far too slow to simulate, and nobody knows the exact chemistry that started life. The rules echo leading ideas instead.

Energy comes from sunlight in shallow water and from heat at the vents. Energy plus raw molecules make building blocks, faster where it is warm. Blocks join into chains only with little water around, so chains pile up in drying tide pools (wet and dry cycles, as Deamer and Damer propose). Water and strong sunlight break chains again.

Very rarely two chains make a copier: a chain that helps make more of itself from blocks. Too few copiers and they die out; enough together and they spread as self-copying patches (Gray-Scott-like kinetics).

Hot vents make oils. Where oils crowd, they curl into bubbles with a skin. A bubble traps the chains and copiers around it and lets blocks and energy in. Bubbles full of chains and copiers pull in oils faster (Chen, Roberts and Szostak, 2004), so they grow and split faster. Nothing tells a bubble to keep copiers: those that do simply leave more daughters. That is the bridge to the first cells.

The Earth itself (land, sea, tides, sun, heat, currents) lives in lib/earth.js so later chapters, with cells, plants and animals, can live in the same world.
