Nervous systems · neurons as a living population

Each dish is a slice of brain tissue where neurons are the organisms. They wire themselves to keep a steady firing rate, compete for growth factor and energy, get pruned by glia, die, and are replaced from a stem-cell niche. Nothing is placed or removed from outside: every change is a cell reacting to what it senses nearby. Change one condition per dish and compare.

What to look for

  • Wiring itself, then pruning. Unconnected cells fire too little, so they sprout. Synapses overshoot to three or four times their final number in the first few thousand steps, then fall as cells die, homeostasis trims and glia engulf the unused ones.
  • Death by competition. More neurons start than the tissue can support, and a third to a half of them die, as in real development. How many survive depends on the growth factor supply, not on a fixed number: compare "Scarce growth factor" with "Full tissue".
  • Repair. Use "Same lesion in every dish". Cells around the hole lose about a quarter of their inputs and usually rebuild them within a couple of hundred steps. Without microglia the debris stays, gets in the way of new contacts, and recovery is slower.
  • Turnover. With neurogenesis the tissue keeps replacing what it loses, and newborns head for injuries along blood vessels. Without it the population slowly shrinks.
